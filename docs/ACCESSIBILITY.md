# Accessibility

Target: WCAG 2.2 level AA on every view. This document records how each requirement is met, how
it is checked, and what has not been checked.

## Views

| View                | Address          | Needs JavaScript | Needs a graphics card |
| ------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- | --------------------- |
| 3D reconstruction   | `/`              | Yes              | For the image only    |
| The night in words  | `/narrative/`    | No               | No                    |
| About, with support | `/about/`        | No               | No                    |
| Legal Notice        | `/legal-notice/` | No               | No                    |

The reconstruction stays usable when the 3D scene cannot start: the timeline, the "Now",
"Sources" and "Narrative" tabs and the flooding diagram are HTML. "The night in words" is the
complete text alternative and needs no script at all.

## Known limits

Measured by the scripted audit and left as they are; none of them loses content or function.

- **Ring of the 3D view over a bright image.** The ring is drawn inside the canvas. Against the
  colours of the page it is at 10:1 or more, but with the view "On the bridge" at 11:40 p.m.,
  which faces a lit bulkhead, most of the pixels beside its left and right sides are under 3:1
  (lowest 1.55:1); its bottom side stays at 7.5:1 or more.
- **Ring of the selected tab.** The bottom side of the ring lies against the accent underline
  of the tab, at 1.1:1 on that face; its other face and the three other sides are at 10:1.
- **Text spacing of criterion 1.4.12.** No text is cut or overlaps. At 1280 px the view
  controls scroll inside their box once the operating instructions are open, and at 320 px the
  night in words becomes 350 px wide because of its table of counts.
- **Narrative below 64 rem.** In the single-column layout the list of the "Narrative" tab does
  not follow playback: the page is not moved, and the event in progress is marked in the list.
- **Key moments with the pointer.** A key moment that shows only in part is brought wholly into
  its row by the keyboard focus, not by the pointer: the row is scrolled by the wheel, by touch
  or with its scroll bar.

## Checks that need a person

Not done. They are to be carried out before the site is announced. The names and texts quoted
are those of the application.

### Screen readers

With NVDA and Firefox, VoiceOver and Safari (macOS and iOS), TalkBack and Chrome.

1. List the landmarks and headings of `/`: banner, navigation "Site", main, regions "3D scene",
   "Timeline" and "Description of the scene", navigation "Key moments", content information;
   one `h1`.
2. From the top of `/`, the first two stops are "Skip to the timeline" and "Skip to the
   description of the scene"; each moves the reading to its region.
3. On the 3D view: name "3D view of the ship", then the sentence on the state of the ship and
   the operating instructions. Tab and Shift+Tab always leave it.
4. In "View" choose "Around the ship": the group "Move the camera" offers "Turn left", "Turn
   right", "Tilt up", "Tilt down", "Zoom in", "Zoom out"; no button is read without a name.
   "Controls of the 3D view" is announced collapsed, then expanded.
5. In a narrow window the playback buttons show an icon only and are still read "Go to the
   collision, 11:40 p.m.", "Back 1 min", "Play", "Forward 1 min", "Go to the wreck on the
   seabed", "Run time backward". The slider "Time of the night, ship's time" reads a time such
   as "11:41 p.m., 1 min after the collision", never a number of seconds.
6. Activate "Go to 2:15 a.m. The bow plunges; the bridge goes under": one announcement about a
   second later, the time then the state of the ship. Then "Play" at ×60: announcements such
   as "2:17 a.m. The hull breaks apart.", never two within four seconds, never the clock read
   continuously.
7. Tabs: "Now", "Sources", "Narrative" with the arrow keys, each announced with its position
   and selected state; Tab enters the panel.
8. Reload `/`: "Loading the 3D scene…" is spoken once. With WebGL disabled in the browser, the
   message that the scene could not be started is spoken, and the timeline and the tabs still
   work.
9. At 1:50 a.m. in "Now": the figure of the water in the hull is read with its caption, then
   the table "Water in each watertight compartment" by row and column; the levels of certainty
   are read as a word ("Established", "Estimated", "Debated").
10. Static pages: one `h1` each, the current page announced in the menu, the table of counts
    of the night in words read with its headers, the four payment links read with "opens in a
    new tab".

Points seen in the code and to be listened for: the value of the slider changes by the minute
while an arrow key moves it by 15 seconds, so several presses in a row can read the same value;
the sentence on the state of the ship is in the description of the 3D view, in the page and at
the head of the "Now" tab, so it can be heard more than once; nothing announces that the scene
is ready after "Loading the 3D scene…".

### Voice control and switch

With Voice Control (macOS, iOS), Voice Access (Windows, Android) and a switch or sequential
scanning.

1. Saying the visible label activates the control: "Play", "Pause", "Back 1 min", "Forward 1
   min", "Collision", "Wreck", "Backward", "Reset view", "Now", "Sources", "Narrative".
2. "View", "Detail" and "Speed" open their list and apply the choice.
3. The step buttons of the camera and, in a narrow window, the playback buttons show no text:
   they answer to their names ("Turn left", "Zoom in", …) and to the numbers overlay.
4. The time moves without dragging: arrow keys and Page Up on the slider, a key moment, a tap
   on the track.
5. With the free camera, every movement is reached by single activations of "Move forward",
   "Move back", "Turn right", "Tilt down", "Reset view".
6. Nothing starts, expires or changes by itself before "Play"; with the "Narrative" tab open
   during playback in a narrow window, the page stays where it is and "Pause" stays in view.

### Forced colours

With the Windows contrast themes, one dark and one light, in Edge and in Firefox. The style
sheets have no `forced-colors` rule: the expected weak points, read in the code, are named in
each item.

1. Focus ring on every stop of `/` and of `/about/`; on the 3D view the ring is inside the
   image, so check it against the dark scene with the light theme.
2. Borders of the buttons, lists, key moments and payment links. The pressed state of
   "Backward" is shown by colours only and may look like the unpressed one.
3. Selected tab, current page of the menu, current key moment, event in progress in
   "Narrative": each is marked by a colour, a weight or a shadow, which a theme may remove or
   make the same for all.
4. Slider: its track, its elapsed part and the ticks of the key moments are backgrounds, which
   a theme removes; only the thumb may remain.
5. Flooding diagram at 1:50 a.m.: its hull, water and sea line keep the colours of the site;
   check them against the background of the light theme, and against the table below it.
6. View controls laid over the image from 1024 px, and the status "Loading the 3D scene…":
   readable over the dark image with both themes.
